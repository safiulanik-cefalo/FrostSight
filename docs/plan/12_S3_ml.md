Owner: Sohanur. Safiul reviews the label definition and the comparison; Rayhan reviews the bundle YAML.
Stretch scope: start only when M7's definition of done is ticked and `orchestrate` has run green for a week.

Goal: a labelled training table (`ml.labels`), a point-in-time feature table (`ml.features`), their join
(`gold.ml_training_features`), a baseline classifier tracked in MLflow and registered in Unity Catalog
(`frostsight.ml.hazard_model`, aliases `@challenger` and `@champion`), an hourly batch prediction job that
writes `gold.road_segment_predicted_risk` for the 1 h, 3 h and 6 h horizons, an honest comparison against the
deterministic icing score, and a limitations note. No model serving: Free Edition has none worth using and
the product reads gold tables anyway (Free Edition plan, section 4).

Inputs from M7 (handover table in 08_M7 T7.9): `gold.historical_closures` and `gold.incident_summary` keyed by
`road_segment_id`; `gold.road_segment_risk_history` and `gold.road_weather_summary`; the time-split rule
from M2's label doc (train before storm day `D`, test on `D` and after); `docs/benchmarks.md` for the cost of a
feature build. Column contracts: 05_M4 T4.4 (`silver.road_weather_observations`, `silver.road_incidents`),
T4.6 (`silver.road_segments`, `silver.road_weather_stations`, `silver.accidents`,
`silver.avalanche_landslide_events`), 06_M5 T5.2 (`windows.with_trends`) and T5.3 (`risk.with_risk`).

## 0. The ML problem in 15 lines, for a data engineer

1. One row per **catchment and time**: `road_segment_id` is the segment of a weather station (the 30 rows of
   `silver.station_segment_lookup`), `ts` is a 10-minute grid over the winter months. 30 x 144 x 150 days is
   about 650,000 rows. Pandas on one serverless driver handles that; no distributed training is needed.
2. The **label** is binary: did a hazard event start within the next H hours after `ts`? Three targets:
   `hazard_next_1h`, `hazard_next_3h` (any closure, accident or slide) and `road_closure_next_6h` (closures only).
3. **Events** come from three silver tables: `silver.road_incidents` (DATEX, types `closure` and `accident`),
   `silver.accidents` (NVDB 570, `Ulykkesdato` + `klokkeslett`) and `silver.avalanche_landslide_events`
   (NVDB 445, `Skred dato` + `klokkeslett`). Each event is attributed to the nearest station within 15 km.
4. **Class imbalance**: perhaps 1 to 3 % of grid rows are positive for the 3-hour target, under 1 % for 1 hour.
   Accuracy is meaningless (predicting "no" everywhere scores 98 %). We use PR-AUC, precision and recall at a
   chosen threshold, and `class_weight="balanced"` so the learner does not ignore the positives.
5. **Time-based split**, not random: rows 10 minutes apart are near-copies of each other. A random split
   puts one copy in train and the other in test, and the test score is a lie. Train Nov to Jan, validate Feb,
   test Mar of last winter. The model must work on a month it has never seen.
6. **Leakage** is the ML word for "the feature knows the answer". The rule for every feature is: a value at
   time `ts` may only use rows with `event_time <= ts`. The as-of join in T-S3.2 enforces it, and a unit
   test proves it. A label for `ts` uses only rows with `event_time > ts`. The two never overlap.
7. The **features** are what the risk engine already sees (surface and air temperature, dew-point spread,
   humidity, precipitation type, wind, 1 h and 3 h trends), rolling sums over 3 h and 6 h, segment statics
   (elevation, road category, speed limit, length), event history (recent counts, closure rate to date) and
   the calendar (hour, weekday, month).
8. **Two models**: logistic regression (the baseline everybody understands) and gradient-boosted trees
   (`HistGradientBoostingClassifier` from scikit-learn, no extra dependency; LightGBM is a drop-in if wanted).
9. The **comparison** the spec asks for (section 14): the same metrics for `icing_score >= threshold` as a
   predictor on the same test month. The icing score was never trained on incidents, so expect it to lose on
   PR-AUC and still be the more explainable product.
10. **MLflow** records every run (parameters, metrics, the model file). **Unity Catalog** stores the registered
    versions; an alias (`@champion`) is a movable pointer the prediction job loads. Promotion is one call.
11. **Batch prediction**: an hourly job builds the current feature row per catchment (30 rows), loads the
    champion, writes probabilities per horizon with MERGE. No endpoint, no real-time path.
12. **What "probability" means here**: "of grid rows that looked like this last winter, this share was
    followed by a reported event within H hours". Reported. Incident feeds miss events; the model inherits that.
13. **Lead time** (spec section 15) is approximated: for each test event, how many minutes before it did the
    model first cross the threshold?
14. Everything runs on **serverless CPU**. No GPU, no cluster, no feature-store service.
15. If the labels are too sparse to learn from (fewer than about 200 positive 3-hour rows in train), stop at
    T-S3.4 and write that down. A documented negative result is a valid stretch outcome.

## 1. Decisions fixed in this milestone

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of prediction | Station catchment, keyed by the station's `road_segment_id` from the lookup | Only 30 of 20,000 segments have weather readings. Predicting for a segment 40 km from any station would be invention. Events within 15 km of a station are attributed to it |
| Grid | 10 minutes, Nov 1 to Mar 31 of last winter (`--start`, `--end`) | Matches the observation cadence; labels and features share the same `ts` |
| Label window | Event **start** in `(ts, ts + H]`; `road_closure_next_6h` counts `incident_type = 'closure'` and slides with `road_closure` set | Start-based labels are not trivially predicted by "a closure is active now"; that fact is a feature instead |
| Incidents without `end_time` | Irrelevant to labels (start-based). For the "active now" feature, `end_time` is `coalesce(end_time, start_time + 6 h)` | DATEX leaves many situations open; 6 h is the median closure length in `gold.historical_closures` (verify with the query in T-S3.1 step 4) |
| Feature storage | **Plain Delta tables**, not Databricks Feature Engineering (`FeatureLookup`) | The ml-training skill's rule: use Feature Engineering when features are shared across models, served online, or need a managed point-in-time join. None applies: one model, batch only, and our as-of join is 20 lines we can unit-test locally. The client library adds a dependency to every job and Free Edition has no online store to publish to. Revisit if a second model or the S2 API needs the same features |
| Observations for training | `silver.road_weather_observations` **including** replay rows (`_batch_id LIKE 'replay:%'`) | Last winter reaches silver only through the replay harness (S1); ML reads all rows, the scheduled gold job still ignores replay rows (06_M5 decision) |
| Split | `ts < 2026-02-01` train, Feb validation, Mar test (`--train-end`, `--val-end`) | One winter of data; the test month must be unseen and contain storms |
| Models | Logistic regression baseline, then `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, `class_weight="balanced"` | No extra package on serverless; handles NaN natively |
| Registered model | One `frostsight.ml.hazard_model` (a pyfunc wrapper holding one sklearn estimator and threshold per target); the three sklearn estimators are also logged with `mlflow.sklearn.log_model` in the same run | One artifact, one alias, one load in the prediction job; the per-horizon flavours stay inspectable in the run |
| Threshold | Per target, the validation F1 maximiser, stored in the model | The product shows a level, not a number; a stored threshold makes `predicted_level` reproducible |
| Prediction table | `gold.road_segment_predicted_risk`, MERGE on `(road_segment_id, ts, horizon_h)`, hourly | Spec section 7.3 name; idempotent re-runs |

```python
"""Hazard labels on a 10-minute grid per station catchment. Rule: a label at ts uses only events with event_time > ts."""
from __future__ import annotations

from pyspark.sql import DataFrame
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

CATCHMENT_KM = 15.0
GRID_MINUTES = 10
TARGETS = {"hazard_next_1h": (1, False), "hazard_next_3h": (3, False), "road_closure_next_6h": (6, True)}  # (hours, closures only)


def _lon(wkt):   # shapely writes 'POINT (18.95 69.65)'
    return F.regexp_extract(wkt, r"POINT Z? ?\(([-\d.]+) ([-\d.]+)", 1).cast("double")


def _lat(wkt):
    return F.regexp_extract(wkt, r"POINT Z? ?\(([-\d.]+) ([-\d.]+)", 2).cast("double")


def _haversine_km(lat1, lon1, lat2, lon2):
    return F.expr(f"2*6371*asin(sqrt(pow(sin(radians({lat2}-{lat1})/2),2)"
                  f"+cos(radians({lat1}))*cos(radians({lat2}))*pow(sin(radians({lon2}-{lon1})/2),2)))")


def events(incidents: DataFrame, accidents: DataFrame, slides: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    """One row per event: event_id, kind, event_time, end_time, latitude, longitude, is_closure."""
    inc = (incidents.where(F.col("incident_type").isin("closure", "accident"))
           .select(F.concat(F.lit("inc:"), "incident_id").alias("event_id"), F.col("incident_type").alias("kind"),
                   F.col("start_time").alias("event_time"), "end_time", "latitude", "longitude",
                   (F.col("incident_type") == "closure").alias("is_closure")))
    acc = accidents.select(F.concat(F.lit("acc:"), F.col("accident_id").cast("string")).alias("event_id"),
                           F.lit("accident").alias("kind"), "event_time", F.lit(None).cast("timestamp").alias("end_time"),
                           _lat("wkt_4326").alias("latitude"), _lon("wkt_4326").alias("longitude"), F.lit(False).alias("is_closure"))
    ava = slides.select(F.concat(F.lit("ava:"), F.col("event_id").cast("string")).alias("event_id"),
                        F.lit("slide").alias("kind"), "event_time", F.lit(None).cast("timestamp").alias("end_time"),
                        _lat("wkt_4326").alias("latitude"), _lon("wkt_4326").alias("longitude"),
                        F.upper(F.coalesce("road_closure", F.lit(""))).rlike("JA|STENGT").alias("is_closure"))  # verify: 2344 values
    return (inc.unionByName(acc).unionByName(ava)
            .where("event_time IS NOT NULL AND latitude IS NOT NULL AND longitude IS NOT NULL"))


def assign_catchment(ev: DataFrame, stations: DataFrame, lookup: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    """Nearest station within CATCHMENT_KM; its segment id becomes the event's road_segment_id."""
    st = (stations.join(lookup.select("station_id", "road_segment_id"), "station_id")
          .select("station_id", "road_segment_id", F.col("latitude").alias("s_lat"), F.col("longitude").alias("s_lon")))
    d = (ev.crossJoin(st).withColumn("d_km", _haversine_km("latitude", "longitude", "s_lat", "s_lon"))
         .where(F.col("d_km") <= CATCHMENT_KM))
    return (d.groupBy(*ev.columns).agg(F.min_by(F.struct("station_id", "road_segment_id", "d_km"), "d_km").alias("c"))
            .select(*ev.columns, "c.station_id", "c.road_segment_id", "c.d_km"))


def grid(lookup: DataFrame, start: str, end: str) -> DataFrame:
    """(station_id, road_segment_id, ts) for every 10 minutes in [start, end]. One station per segment."""
    seg = lookup.select("station_id", "road_segment_id").dropDuplicates(["road_segment_id"])
    ts = seg.sparkSession.sql(
        f"SELECT explode(sequence(timestamp'{start}', timestamp'{end}', interval {GRID_MINUTES} minutes)) AS ts")
    return seg.crossJoin(ts)


def label_grid(g: DataFrame, ev: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    """Adds the three targets and event_ids (events starting within 6 h) to every grid row."""
    e = ev.select("road_segment_id", "event_id", "event_time", "is_closure")
    j = g.join(e, (g.road_segment_id == e.road_segment_id) & (e.event_time > g.ts)
               & (e.event_time <= g.ts + F.expr("INTERVAL 6 HOURS")), "left").drop(e.road_segment_id)
    aggs = []
    for name, (hours, closures_only) in TARGETS.items():
        hit = F.col("event_time") <= F.col("ts") + F.expr(f"INTERVAL {hours} HOURS")
        if closures_only:
            hit = hit & F.col("is_closure")
        aggs.append(F.max(F.when(hit, 1).otherwise(0)).alias(name))
    return (j.groupBy("station_id", "road_segment_id", "ts")
            .agg(*aggs, F.collect_set("event_id").alias("event_ids")))
```

### T-S3.2 Features and the training table      owner: Sohanur
Why: the features must be computable identically for last winter (training) and for "now" (prediction),
with nothing from the future. One module does both.
Do:
  1. Create `src/frostsight/features.py`:

```python
"""Point-in-time features on the grid. Rule: a feature at ts uses only rows with event_time <= ts."""
from __future__ import annotations

from pyspark.sql import DataFrame, Window
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

MAX_OBS_AGE_MIN = 60
OBS = {  # observation columns carried as-of, with their types
    "air_temperature_c": "double", "road_surface_temperature_c": "double", "dew_point_c": "double",
    "humidity_pct": "double", "precipitation_type": "string", "precipitation_intensity_mm_h": "double",
    "wind_speed_ms": "double", "temperature_change_1h": "double", "temperature_change_3h": "double",
}
PRECIP = ("SNOW", "SLEET", "FREEZING_RAIN", "RAIN", "DRIZZLE", "NONE")
ROAD_CAT = ("E", "R", "F")
FEATURE_COLS = (
    ["air_temperature_c", "road_surface_temperature_c", "dew_point_spread_c", "humidity_pct",
     "precipitation_intensity_mm_h", "wind_speed_ms", "temperature_change_1h", "temperature_change_3h",
     "surface_change_3h_c", "precip_3h_mm", "precip_6h_mm", "surface_min_6h_c", "obs_age_min"]
    + [f"precip_{p}" for p in PRECIP]
    + ["elevation_m", "speed_limit", "length_m"] + [f"road_cat_{c}" for c in ROAD_CAT]
    + ["recent_events_6h", "recent_events_24h", "active_closure_now", "closure_rate_per_year", "event_rate_per_year"]
    + ["hour", "dow", "month"])


def _t(df):
    return df.withColumn("_t", F.col("ts").cast("long"))


def as_of_observations(grid: DataFrame, obs: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    """For every (station_id, ts): the latest observation with event_time <= ts, at most MAX_OBS_AGE_MIN old,
    plus rolling 3 h / 6 h aggregates. Pattern: union grid rows (measures null) with observation rows, then
    last(ignorenulls=True) over a range window that ends at the current row. Nothing after ts is in the frame."""
    o = obs.select("station_id", F.col("event_time").alias("ts"), F.lit(0).alias("is_grid"),
                   *[F.col(c).alias(f"o_{c}") for c in OBS])
    g = grid.select("station_id", "ts", F.lit(1).alias("is_grid"),
                    *[F.lit(None).cast(t).alias(f"o_{c}") for c, t in OBS.items()])
    u = _t(g.unionByName(o))
    w1 = Window.partitionBy("station_id").orderBy("_t").rangeBetween(-MAX_OBS_AGE_MIN * 60, 0)
    w3 = Window.partitionBy("station_id").orderBy("_t").rangeBetween(-3 * 3600, 0)
    w6 = Window.partitionBy("station_id").orderBy("_t").rangeBetween(-6 * 3600, 0)
    out = (u.withColumn("precip_3h_mm", F.sum(F.col("o_precipitation_intensity_mm_h") / 6.0).over(w3))
             .withColumn("precip_6h_mm", F.sum(F.col("o_precipitation_intensity_mm_h") / 6.0).over(w6))
             .withColumn("surface_min_6h_c", F.min("o_road_surface_temperature_c").over(w6))
             .withColumn("surface_change_3h_c", F.last("o_road_surface_temperature_c", ignorenulls=True).over(w3)
                         - F.first("o_road_surface_temperature_c", ignorenulls=True).over(w3))
             .withColumn("obs_time", F.last(F.when(F.col("is_grid") == 0, F.col("ts")), ignorenulls=True).over(w1)))
    for c in OBS:
        out = out.withColumn(c, F.last(f"o_{c}", ignorenulls=True).over(w1))
    return (out.where("is_grid = 1")
            .withColumn("obs_age_min", (F.col("_t") - F.col("obs_time").cast("long")) / 60.0)
            .withColumn("dew_point_spread_c", F.col("air_temperature_c") - F.col("dew_point_c"))
            .drop("_t", "is_grid", "obs_time", *[f"o_{c}" for c in OBS]))


def with_event_history(df: DataFrame, ev: DataFrame, season_start: str) -> DataFrame:
    """Counts of events with event_time <= ts (same union-and-range-window pattern, keyed by segment)."""
    e = ev.select("road_segment_id", F.col("event_time").alias("ts"), F.lit(0).alias("is_grid"), F.lit(1).alias("ev"),
                  F.col("is_closure").cast("int").alias("cl"),
                  F.coalesce("end_time", F.col("event_time") + F.expr("INTERVAL 6 HOURS")).alias("closure_end"))
    g = df.select("road_segment_id", "ts", F.lit(1).alias("is_grid"), F.lit(0).alias("ev"), F.lit(0).alias("cl"),
                  F.lit(None).cast("timestamp").alias("closure_end"))
    u = _t(g.unionByName(e))
    by_seg = Window.partitionBy("road_segment_id").orderBy("_t")
    w6, w24, wall = (by_seg.rangeBetween(-6 * 3600, 0), by_seg.rangeBetween(-24 * 3600, 0),
                     by_seg.rangeBetween(Window.unboundedPreceding, 0))
    days = F.greatest((F.col("_t") - F.lit(season_start).cast("timestamp").cast("long")) / 86400.0, F.lit(1.0))
    h = (u.withColumn("recent_events_6h", F.sum("ev").over(w6))
          .withColumn("recent_events_24h", F.sum("ev").over(w24))
          # A closure that started <= 24 h ago and has not ended at ts. The comparison with ts happens outside the
          # window: inside a frame, `ts` would be each frame row's own time, not the current grid row's.
          .withColumn("active_closure_now", (F.max(F.when(F.col("cl") == 1, F.col("closure_end"))).over(w24) > F.col("ts"))
                      .cast("int"))
          .withColumn("active_closure_now", F.coalesce("active_closure_now", F.lit(0)))
          .withColumn("closure_rate_per_year", F.sum("cl").over(wall) / days * 365.0)
          .withColumn("event_rate_per_year", F.sum("ev").over(wall) / days * 365.0)
          .where("is_grid = 1").drop("_t", "is_grid", "ev", "cl", "closure_end"))
    return df.join(h, ["road_segment_id", "ts"], "left")


def with_statics_and_calendar(df: DataFrame, segments: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    s = segments.select("road_segment_id", "elevation_m", "road_category", "speed_limit", "length_m")
    out = df.join(s, "road_segment_id", "left")
    for p in PRECIP:
        out = out.withColumn(f"precip_{p}", (F.coalesce("precipitation_type", F.lit("NONE")) == p).cast("int"))
    for c in ROAD_CAT:
        out = out.withColumn(f"road_cat_{c}", (F.upper("road_category") == c).cast("int"))
    return (out.withColumn("hour", F.hour("ts")).withColumn("dow", F.dayofweek("ts")).withColumn("month", F.month("ts")))


def build_features(grid: DataFrame, obs: DataFrame, ev: DataFrame, segments: DataFrame, season_start: str) -> DataFrame:
    """grid: station_id, road_segment_id, ts. obs: silver observations with trend columns. ev: ml.events."""
    f = as_of_observations(grid, obs)
    f = with_event_history(f, ev, season_start)
    f = with_statics_and_calendar(f, segments)
    return f.select("road_segment_id", "station_id", "ts", "precipitation_type", *FEATURE_COLS)
```

     Why S3 does not read S2 at all (`gold.historical_baselines`, `gold.road_segment_anomalies`): the
     baselines are computed over the whole of last winter, which is also this model's train, validation and
     test period, so a baseline feature at `ts` in January would carry February and March readings and
     closures into training. That is leakage, and it would inflate the test PR-AUC. The expanding-window rates above are point-in-time correct by construction. When S2 exists and a
     second winter of data does too, add a `FeatureLookup`-free left join on `road_segment_id` here.

```python
"""build_features: ml.features on the label grid, then gold.ml_training_features = labels JOIN features."""
import argparse

from pyspark.sql import SparkSession
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

from frostsight.features import build_features
from frostsight.windows import with_trends

spark = SparkSession.builder.getOrCreate()
p = argparse.ArgumentParser()
p.add_argument("--catalog", required=True)
p.add_argument("--start", default="2025-11-01 00:00:00")       # season_start for the rate features
a = p.parse_args()
c = a.catalog

labels = spark.read.table(f"{c}.ml.labels")
grid = labels.select("station_id", "road_segment_id", "ts")
obs = with_trends(spark.read.table(f"{c}.silver.road_weather_observations")
                  .where(F.col("event_time") >= F.lit(a.start).cast("timestamp") - F.expr("INTERVAL 7 HOURS")))  # replay rows included
feats = build_features(grid, obs, spark.read.table(f"{c}.ml.events"), spark.read.table(f"{c}.silver.road_segments"), a.start)
```

```python
"""One pyfunc holding one sklearn classifier and one threshold per target. Output: p_<target>, thr_<target>."""
from __future__ import annotations

import mlflow
import pandas as pd

from frostsight.features import FEATURE_COLS

TARGET_HOURS = {"hazard_next_1h": 1, "hazard_next_3h": 3, "road_closure_next_6h": 6}


class HazardModel(mlflow.pyfunc.PythonModel):
    def __init__(self, models: dict | None = None):
        self.models = models or {}          # {target: (fitted estimator, threshold)}

    def predict(self, context, model_input: pd.DataFrame, params=None) -> pd.DataFrame:
        X = model_input[FEATURE_COLS].astype("float64")
        out = pd.DataFrame(index=model_input.index)
        for target, (est, thr) in self.models.items():
            out[f"p_{target}"] = est.predict_proba(X)[:, 1]
            out[f"thr_{target}"] = thr
        return out
```

```python
# Data and the time split. One pandas frame: 650k rows x ~35 columns is about 200 MB, fine on the serverless driver.
df = spark.read.table(f"{a.catalog}.gold.ml_training_features").toPandas().sort_values("ts")
tr = df[df.ts < pd.Timestamp(a.train_end)]

print({k: len(v) for k, v in X.items()}, {t: int(tr[t].sum()) for t in TARGETS})


def best_threshold(y, p):
    prec, rec, thr = precision_recall_curve(y, p)
    f1 = 2 * prec[:-1] * rec[:-1] / np.clip(prec[:-1] + rec[:-1], 1e-9, None)
    return float(thr[int(np.argmax(f1))])


def lead_time_min(rows: pd.DataFrame, p: np.ndarray, thr: float, hours: int, events: pd.DataFrame) -> float | None:
    """Median minutes between the first grid row scoring >= thr and the event it precedes, within the horizon."""
    hit = rows.loc[p >= thr, ["road_segment_id", "ts", "event_ids"]].explode("event_ids").dropna()
    if hit.empty:
        return None
    m = hit.merge(events, left_on="event_ids", right_on="event_id")
    m = m[(m.event_time > m.ts) & (m.event_time <= m.ts + pd.Timedelta(hours=hours))]
    first = m.groupby("event_id").ts.min()
    lead = (events.set_index("event_id").loc[first.index, "event_time"] - first).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    return float(lead.median()) if len(lead) else None


events_pd = spark.read.table(f"{a.catalog}.ml.events").select("event_id", "event_time").toPandas()

# COMMAND ----------
models, summary = {}, {}
with mlflow.start_run(run_name=f"hazard_{a.val_end}") as run:
    mlflow.log_params({"train_end": a.train_end, "val_end": a.val_end, "rows_train": len(tr), "rows_val": len(va),
                       "rows_test": len(te), "features": len(FEATURE_COLS)})
    for target in TARGETS:
        y_tr, y_va, y_te = tr[target].values, va[target].values, te[target].values
        cands = {
            "lr": make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), StandardScaler(),
                                LogisticRegression(class_weight="balanced", max_iter=2000)),
            "hgb": HistGradientBoostingClassifier(class_weight="balanced", learning_rate=0.05, max_iter=400,
                                                  max_leaf_nodes=31, early_stopping=True, random_state=7),
        }
        scores = {}
        for name, est in cands.items():
            est.fit(X["tr"], y_tr)
            scores[name] = average_precision_score(y_va, est.predict_proba(X["va"])[:, 1])
            mlflow.log_metric(f"val_pr_auc_{target}_{name}", scores[name])
        chosen = max(scores, key=scores.get)
        est = cands[chosen]
        thr = best_threshold(y_va, est.predict_proba(X["va"])[:, 1])
        p_te = est.predict_proba(X["te"])[:, 1]
        m = {"model": chosen, "threshold": thr, "positive_rate_train": float(y_tr.mean()),
             "test_pr_auc": float(average_precision_score(y_te, p_te)),
             "test_precision": float(precision_score(y_te, p_te >= thr, zero_division=0)),
             "test_recall": float(recall_score(y_te, p_te >= thr, zero_division=0)),
             "test_lead_time_min": lead_time_min(te, p_te, thr, TARGET_HOURS[target], events_pd)}
        for k, v in m.items():
            if isinstance(v, float):
                mlflow.log_metric(f"{k}_{target}", v)
        mlflow.set_tag(f"model_{target}", chosen)
        mlflow.sklearn.log_model(est, name=f"sklearn_{target}")       # per-horizon flavour, inspectable in the run
        # Monotonicity smoke test: colder surface must not lower the mean probability (logged, not asserted)
        base = X["te"].sample(min(2000, len(te)), random_state=1)
        cold, warm = base.assign(road_surface_temperature_c=-5.0), base.assign(road_surface_temperature_c=5.0)
        mlflow.log_metric(f"mono_cold_minus_warm_{target}",
                          float(est.predict_proba(cold)[:, 1].mean() - est.predict_proba(warm)[:, 1].mean()))
        models[target], summary[target] = (est, thr), m

    wrapper = HazardModel(models)
    example = X["te"].head(5)
    info = mlflow.pyfunc.log_model(
        name="model", python_model=wrapper, input_example=example,
        signature=infer_signature(example, wrapper.predict(None, example)),
        registered_model_name=MODEL_NAME,
        pip_requirements=[f"mlflow=={mlflow.__version__}", "scikit-learn", "pandas", "numpy"])
    version = info.registered_model_version
    client.set_registered_model_alias(MODEL_NAME, "challenger", version)
    client.set_model_version_tag(MODEL_NAME, version, "test_month", a.val_end)

# COMMAND ----------
# Promotion: champion only if the new version beats the current champion on the same test month.
if a.promote:
    new = summary["hazard_next_3h"]["test_pr_auc"]
    try:
        champ = client.get_model_version_by_alias(MODEL_NAME, "champion")
        old = client.get_run(champ.run_id).data.metrics.get("test_pr_auc_hazard_next_3h", -1.0)
    except Exception:
        old = -1.0
    if new > old:
        client.set_registered_model_alias(MODEL_NAME, "champion", version)
        print(f"champion -> v{version} (pr_auc {new:.3f} > {old:.3f})")
    else:
        print(f"kept champion (pr_auc {old:.3f} >= {new:.3f}); v{version} stays challenger")
print(json.dumps({"version": version, "run_id": run.info.run_id, **{t: summary[t] for t in TARGETS}}, indent=1, default=str))
```

```python
"""predict: current feature row per catchment -> champion model -> gold.road_segment_predicted_risk (MERGE).
30 rows per horizon; scoring happens in pandas on the driver. spark_udf would be the tool at 30,000 rows."""
import argparse
from datetime import datetime, timezone

import mlflow
from delta.tables import DeltaTable
from mlflow.tracking import MlflowClient
from pyspark.sql import SparkSession
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

from frostsight.features import FEATURE_COLS, build_features
from frostsight.ml_model import TARGET_HOURS
from frostsight.windows import with_trends

spark = SparkSession.builder.getOrCreate()
DDL = """road_segment_id STRING, ts TIMESTAMP, horizon_h INT, target STRING, probability DOUBLE, threshold DOUBLE,
         predicted_level STRING, model_version STRING, scored_at TIMESTAMP"""


def level(p: float, thr: float) -> str:
    if p >= min(1.0, 2 * thr):
        return "VERY_HIGH"
    if p >= thr:
        return "HIGH"
    if p >= thr / 2:
        return "MEDIUM"
    return "LOW"


def main() -> None:
    p = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    p.add_argument("--catalog", required=True)
    p.add_argument("--season-start", default="2026-11-01 00:00:00")   # this winter, for the rate features
    p.add_argument("--model-name", default="hazard_model")            # bare name, as registered by train (T-S3.7)
    a = p.parse_args()
    c = a.catalog
    mlflow.set_registry_uri("databricks-uc")
    name = f"{c}.ml.{a.model_name}"
    version = MlflowClient(registry_uri="databricks-uc").get_model_version_by_alias(name, "champion").version
    model = mlflow.pyfunc.load_model(f"models:/{name}@champion")

    now = datetime.now(timezone.utc).replace(second=0, microsecond=0)
    ts = now.replace(minute=now.minute - now.minute % 10)
    lookup = spark.read.table(f"{c}.silver.station_segment_lookup").select("station_id", "road_segment_id").dropDuplicates(["road_segment_id"])
    grid = lookup.withColumn("ts", F.lit(ts))
    obs = with_trends(spark.read.table(f"{c}.silver.road_weather_observations")
                      .where((F.col("event_time") >= F.lit(ts) - F.expr("INTERVAL 7 HOURS")) & ~F.col("_batch_id").startswith("replay:")))
    ev = spark.read.table(f"{c}.ml.events").where(F.col("event_time") >= F.lit(a.season_start).cast("timestamp"))   # refreshed by the train job; live incidents via T-S3.7 step 3
    feats = build_features(grid, obs, ev, spark.read.table(f"{c}.silver.road_segments"), a.season_start)
    feats.write.format("delta").mode("overwrite").option("overwriteSchema", "true").saveAsTable(f"{c}.ml.features_latest")

    pdf = feats.toPandas()
    pred = model.predict(pdf[FEATURE_COLS].astype("float64"))          # the signature was logged from float64 columns
    rows = []
    for i, seg in enumerate(pdf.road_segment_id):
        for target, hours in TARGET_HOURS.items():
            prob, thr = float(pred[f"p_{target}"].iloc[i]), float(pred[f"thr_{target}"].iloc[i])
            rows.append((seg, ts, hours, target, prob, thr, level(prob, thr), str(version), now))
    out = spark.createDataFrame(rows, DDL)

    spark.sql(f"CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS {c}.gold.road_segment_predicted_risk ({DDL}) CLUSTER BY (road_segment_id, ts)")
    (DeltaTable.forName(spark, f"{c}.gold.road_segment_predicted_risk").alias("t")
     .merge(out.alias("s"), "t.road_segment_id = s.road_segment_id AND t.ts = s.ts AND t.horizon_h = s.horizon_h")
     .whenMatchedUpdateAll().whenNotMatchedInsertAll().execute())
    print(f"scored {len(rows)} rows at {ts} with v{version}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## 5. Limitations (copy into `docs/ml_evaluation.md`)

1. **Incident feeds are incomplete.** DATEX situations, NVDB accidents and slides record what was reported,
   not what happened. A slippery road nobody crashed on is a negative label. Recall is a lower bound and
   precision is pessimistic for both the icing score and the model (spec section 15 and 30).
2. **Label noise.** Accident times come from `Ulykkesdato` plus a clock time that may be missing (row dropped)
   or rounded to the hour; DATEX `start_time` is the report time, not the onset; roadworks typed as closures
   are hazards to nobody. The 15 km catchment attributes an event to a station that may sit in a different
   microclimate.
3. **One winter, one county.** Training, validation and test come from the same season in Troms. The model
   has never seen a different winter, a coastal county or a road without a nearby station. Do not apply it
   outside the pilot without retraining.
4. **Thirty catchments, not twenty thousand segments.** Predictions exist only where a weather station is.
   The map's other segments show the deterministic score derived from the nearest station, and no prediction.
5. **No serving, no real-time path.** Predictions are refreshed hourly from observations up to 60 minutes
   old; the horizon starts at the grid time `ts`, which the row carries. A user asking "in 3 hours" at 14:50
   reads a row scored at 14:35 for `ts = 14:30`.
6. **The deterministic score stays the headline.** Its drivers are explainable; the model's are not (a
   feature-importance plot is not an explanation a road operator can act on). The comparison table says which
   scores better on last winter's reports; it does not say which is right.
7. **Not an official warning service.** Nothing here replaces Statens vegvesen's traffic information.
